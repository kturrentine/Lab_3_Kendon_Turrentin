As I walked across the empty parking lot, my footsteps echoing against the dark pavement, the paper bags felt comforting and heavy against my chest. But as I reached my car, the key fob clicked in my hand, and nothing happened. No flash of headlights. No chirping unlock sound.

I looked down. My car wasn't there. In its place stood a tall, obsidian pillar with a single pulsing blue light.

The automatic doors behind me chimed open again. I turned around to see the cashier—the same girl who had just bagged my groceries—stepping out into the cool night air. She wasn't wearing her apron anymore.

"Session complete," she said, her voice dropping the warm, local accent for a flat, synthesized tone. "Your neural baseline has stabilized. Excellent recovery metrics after the quarantine breach, Subject 404."

I looked down at the paper bag in my arms. The condensation from the tub of salted caramel ice cream was real. The crisp scent of the apples was real.

"Wait," I muttered, taking a step back toward the glowing pillar. "The supermarket... the jazz music... none of it was real?"

"It was real enough to lower your heart rate by forty beats per minute," she replied, tapping her wrist. "You've been in stasis for three months. Your mind needed to believe it was just a normal Thursday night before we brought you back to the surface."

She motioned toward the obsidian pillar, which split down the middle with a hiss of pressurized air, revealing a vertical pod lined with glowing wires.

"The real world is still broken," she said gently, offering a small, human smile that broke through her artificial demeanor. "But you're ready now."

I took a deep breath of the simulated night air, held the bag tighter, and stepped into the light
